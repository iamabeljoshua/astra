TRUTH KIT. These functions compute reality and are already available. Use them instead of your own geometry whenever the topic is one of these, and do not place, tilt, or orbit the moving body yourself.

moon_orbit(self, moon, earth, radius, sun_dir, phases) with phases={'moment-id':'new'|'waxing-crescent'|'first-quarter'|'waxing-gibbous'|'full'|'waning-gibbous'|'last-quarter'|'waning-crescent'} returns o. It moves the Moon counterclockwise seen from north, reaches each named phase on its moment, and sets the light along sun_dir. o.phase_name(t) gives the true phase name, so use it for any phase label, with t=self.time or T['moment-id']. o.lit_fraction(t) gives the lit share of the disk seen from Earth, o.angle(t) the degrees past new. o.view(radius) returns a flat group showing the Moon as seen from Earth at this moment, always right, so use it rather than drawing a phase disk; add it with self.add_fixed_in_frame_mobjects in 3D. Make Earth and Moon with self.part('earth', ...) and self.part('moon', ...).

seasons(self, earth, sun, radius, tilt_deg=23.44, moments={'moment-id':'june-solstice'|'september-equinox'|'december-solstice'|'march-equinox'}) returns o. It moves Earth counterclockwise round the Sun and holds the axis fixed in space at the true tilt, with the north pole leaning toward the Sun at the June solstice. o.axis_line(length) returns the axis to add. o.season(t,'north'|'south') gives that hemisphere's season, o.subsolar_lat(t) the latitude where the Sun is overhead, o.north_leans_to_sun(t) a boolean, o.position(t) Earth's place. Make Earth and Sun with self.part('earth', ...) and self.part('sun', ...).
