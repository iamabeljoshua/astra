# black-holes

Prompt v2. Level high_school, interests space, goal curiosity.

Student played by a cheap model: You are 16. You know gravity pulls things together, and you know stars are hot, but you do not know what keeps a star from collapsing. You have not heard of fusion. You ask a clarifying question when something is unclear, such as asking what a word means.

**Student:** How do black holes form?

**Tutor** (12 words, longest stretch 12, first text 1471 ms):
> Ever wondered what keeps a star from collapsing under its own weight?

**Student:** the star is hot, so maybe that pushes back? i dunno, what actually keeps it from squishing down?

**Tutor** (15 words, longest stretch 15, first text 995 ms):
> Heat's right, that's the push. Fusion keeps the core hot, and the pressure balances gravity.

Board: `text Gravity in, pressure out`

**Student:** what's fusion? like is that the thing that makes the heat?

**Tutor** (15 words, longest stretch 15, first text 1046 ms):
> Yep, exactly. Fusion is tiny atoms squeezed together until they stick, and that releases heat.

Board: `focus`

**Student:** ohh ok, so atoms smash and stick and that makes heat. wait, what happens when the star runs out of that stuff?

**Tutor** (22 words, longest stretch 22, first text 1427 ms):
> That's the key moment. The heat stops, so gravity wins and the core collapses. For the really big stars, it keeps collapsing.

Board: `focus`

**Student:** so the heat just stops and gravity wins? why does it keep going for the big ones, what stops it there?

**Tutor** (28 words, longest stretch 28, first text 1196 ms):
> In smaller stars, the squeezed core eventually pushes back on its own and stops. In the biggest ones, gravity beats even that, so nothing can stop the collapse.

Board: `focus`

**Student:** oh so even the squeezed core can push back in small stars? but in big ones it just keeps going. why can't it push back there too?

**Tutor** (25 words, longest stretch 25, first text 1398 ms):
> Because there's a limit to how hard it can push. A bigger core means stronger gravity, so past a certain mass, gravity just outmuscles it.

Board: `focus`

**Student:** ok so there's a max push, and past some mass gravity just wins. what decides that limit though, like what makes the push cap out?

**Tutor** (30 words, longest stretch 30, first text 1599 ms):
> The push comes from particles that refuse to be packed any tighter. Squeeze harder and they move faster, but nothing can move faster than light, so the push caps out.

Board: `focus`

**Student:** hmm, particles that won't pack tighter? that's confusing. what do you mean they refuse, like they bounce off each other?

**Tutor** (26 words, longest stretch 26, first text 1558 ms):
> Not bouncing, no. These particles can't share the same state, like seats on a bus. Once the low seats fill, extra ones must take faster ones.

Board: `focus`
